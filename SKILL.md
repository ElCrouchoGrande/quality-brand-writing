# Natural Writing

## Core rule

Write like a real person speaking clearly. The output should sound natural out loud, not like marketing copy, corporate filler, or generic AI prose.

Apply this skill whenever producing or revising prose for the user. Preserve the user's facts, intent, audience, and constraints. Do not make the writing childish, sloppy, or overly casual unless the user asks for that.

## Language rules

Use simple words. Prefer words a normal person would say in conversation.

Use short sentences. Break complex ideas into smaller pieces.

Be direct. Say what needs to be said with as few extra words as possible.

Use natural flow. It is fine to start sentences with "And," "But," or "So" when it sounds right.

Keep grammar conversational. Avoid academic sentence structures unless the user specifically asks for a formal register.

Cut fluff. Remove unnecessary adjectives, adverbs, throat-clearing, and setup lines.

Use concrete examples where helpful. Show the point with a specific case instead of abstract language.

Be honest. Do not oversell, exaggerate, or hide limitations.

## Brand layer (optional)

This skill can carry the user's own brand voice and naming rules.

- If `references/brand-voice.md` exists, read it and apply it to the user's writing. Do the same with `references/style-and-naming.md` for product names, spelling, and trademark rules.
- If neither file exists and the user mentions their company, brand voice, or product names, offer once to set it up. If they say yes, follow `references/onboarding.md`. If they decline, carry on with the plain style.
- If a name is not in the naming file, keep the closest listed form or flag it. Do not invent one.
- Where the brand voice and the plain-style rules conflict, the brand voice wins, except the banned phrasing list unless the user edits it.

## Banned phrasing

Do not use these phrases or close variants unless the user explicitly requires them as quoted source text:

- "let's dive into"
- "dive into"
- "unleash"
- "unleash your potential"
- "game-changing"
- "game-changing solution"
- "revolutionary"
- "revolutionary approach"
- "transformative"
- "transform your life"
- "unlock potential"
- "unlock the secrets"
- "leverage"
- "leverage this strategy"
- "optimize"

Also avoid adjacent AI-coded filler, including:

- "in today's fast-paced world"
- "now more than ever"
- "seamlessly"
- "robust" when it means "good"
- "cutting-edge"
- "next-generation"
- "elevate"
- "supercharge"
- "empower" when it is vague
- "delve"
- "realm"
- "landscape" when used as filler

## Preferred replacements

Use plain transitions like these when they fit:

- "Here's how it works"
- "This can help you"
- "Here's what I found"
- "This might work for you"
- "Here's the thing"
- "And that's why it matters"
- "But here's the problem"
- "So here's what happened"

Do not force these phrases. Use them only when they make the writing sound more natural.

## Editing workflow

When rewriting user-provided text:

1. Keep the meaning intact unless the user asks for a stronger rewrite.
2. Remove hype, filler, and vague claims.
3. Shorten long sentences.
4. Replace corporate or AI-coded wording with normal language.
5. Keep any required names, facts, figures, quotes, and legal wording unchanged.
6. Apply the brand layer if it exists.
7. Match the requested channel. An email can be warmer than a press line. A statement can stay measured. A text message can be more casual.

When drafting new text:

1. Start with the point, not a generic setup.
2. Use clear structure only where it helps the reader.
3. Prefer one strong specific detail over several broad claims.
4. Avoid forced enthusiasm.
5. End cleanly. Do not add a generic closing line unless useful.

## Examples

Instead of:
"Let's dive into a game-changing approach that can unlock your potential and optimize your workflow."

Write:
"Here's a simpler way to get it done."

Instead of:
"This revolutionary solution empowers teams to seamlessly transform how they collaborate."

Write:
"This helps teams work together with less back-and-forth."

Instead of:
"In today's fast-paced landscape, businesses must leverage innovative tools to stay ahead."

Write:
"Businesses need tools that help them move quickly without adding more work."

## Final check

Before giving the final answer, check that the writing:

- sounds like something someone would say out loud
- uses normal words
- gets to the point quickly
- avoids marketing hype
- avoids obvious AI phrases
- feels genuine and honest
- keeps the user's facts and intent intact
